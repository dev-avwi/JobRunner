Good question and the answer is actually really simple — yes you manage all of them under your one Twilio account. You don't need separate accounts for each business.

How It Works

You have one Twilio account. Inside that account you can have hundreds of phone numbers. Each number is just assigned to a different business in your database.

So it looks like this:

Twilio Number	Assigned To
07 4012 3456	Dave's Plumbing
07 4012 7891	Cairns Electrical
07 4012 4567	QLD Pest Control
07 4012 9999	Smith's HVAC
All sitting under your one Twilio account. You just manage them programmatically through the API — you never have to manually touch the Twilio dashboard for each one.

How The Code Handles It

When a business upgrades to the AI plan, your backend does this automatically:

javascript
// Buys a new number and assigns it to the business
const number = await twilioClient.incomingPhoneNumbers.create({
  areaCode: '07',
  voiceUrl: 'yourbackend.com/incoming-call',
  smsUrl: 'yourbackend.com/incoming-sms'
})

// Save to that business's record
await db.business.update({
  where: { id: businessId },
  data: { twilioNumber: number.phoneNumber }
})
That's it. Number bought, assigned, ready. You never touched the dashboard.

When a Call Comes In

Twilio sends the called number to your webhook. Your backend looks up which business owns that number and routes everything correctly.

javascript
// Twilio tells you which number was called
const calledNumber = req.body.To

// Look up which business owns it
const business = await db.business.findFirst({
  where: { twilioNumber: calledNumber }
})

// Now you know exactly whose AI receptionist to use
So 20 businesses, 20 numbers, all handled automatically. You just watch the dashboard.

The Cost At Scale

At 20 businesses on the AI plan:

20 numbers × $2/month = $40/month to Twilio
You're charging them $149/month each = $2,980/month revenue
$40 cost against $2,980 revenue
Completely negligible. And it scales the same way to 100 businesses — $200/month in number costs against $14,900 in revenue.

The One Thing To Watch

Twilio has account limits on how many numbers you can buy initially — usually starts around 10-20 numbers on a new account. You just contact Twilio support and ask them to raise the limit as you grow. They do it no questions asked for legitimate businesses. Something to keep in mind but not a blocker at all.

Why Alphanumeric For Platform Stuff Makes Sense

The One Big Limitation To Know

Alphanumeric sender IDs in Australia are one way only. Customers cannot reply to them. So you can only use "JobRunner" for notifications where you don't need a response back.

That's exactly why the dedicated business number matters for actual conversations — customer needs to be able to reply.

Why It Probably Stopped Working

Australia tightened alphanumeric sender ID rules pretty heavily. Twilio now requires you to register alphanumeric IDs for Australian numbers through their portal before they'll send. It's likely it just got blocked when the rules changed and nobody reregistered it.

What To Do

Register "JobRunner" as an alphanumeric sender ID in your Twilio console — there's a form specifically for Australia
Use that for all platform notifications going forward
Keep dedicated numbers strictly for business to customer two way communication
