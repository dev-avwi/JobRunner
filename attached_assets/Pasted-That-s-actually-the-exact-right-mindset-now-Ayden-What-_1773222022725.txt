That’s actually the exact right mindset now, Ayden. 👍
What you’re describing is the moment when a product moves from “building mode” → “product mode.” Most founders struggle to make that switch, so you’re already thinking correctly.

Paul basically told you the same thing: stop adding features and focus on reliability and users.

Here’s the best strategy for you right now.

⸻

1️⃣ Freeze Features (Don’t Add Anything New)

For the next 2–3 months, treat the app as if development is frozen.

Only do three things:
	•	fix bugs
	•	improve reliability
	•	improve clarity (small UI tweaks)

No new features unless multiple users request them.

This prevents what developers call feature creep.

⸻

2️⃣ Do Proper Quality Testing

Instead of building, you now break your own app on purpose.

Test things like:

Job workflow
	•	create job
	•	edit job
	•	attach files
	•	complete job

Quote → invoice workflow
	•	create quote
	•	convert to invoice
	•	record payment

File uploads
	•	upload photos
	•	large files
	•	multiple files

Multi-company isolation

Create:

Company A
Company B

Make sure they cannot see each other’s data.

This was one of Paul’s key concerns.

⸻

3️⃣ Stress Test It

Try weird situations:
	•	slow internet
	•	uploading big files
	•	refreshing pages mid-save
	•	creating hundreds of records
	•	multiple users logged in

Real businesses will do unexpected things, so your app needs to survive that.

⸻

4️⃣ Find 5–10 Test Businesses

This is the most important step now.

Paul suggested using people in your network.

Examples:
	•	friends’ parents
	•	sports team families
	•	local tradies
	•	small service businesses

Tell them:

“I built a job management app and I’m letting a few local businesses use it free while I test it.”

Small businesses love trying tools if it helps them run jobs easier.

⸻

5️⃣ Watch Them Use It

This is extremely valuable.

When someone uses your app:

Watch for:
	•	where they get confused
	•	where they click wrong things
	•	where they hesitate
	•	where they say “how do I do this?”

These moments show UX problems, not feature problems.

⸻

6️⃣ Fix Problems, Not Features

When users give feedback, focus on:

✔ making things simpler
✔ reducing steps
✔ improving clarity
✔ fixing bugs

Not adding more tools.

Many great SaaS products succeed because they are simple and reliable.

⸻

7️⃣ Build Trust in the System

Businesses will only rely on software if they trust it.

That means:
	•	data never disappears
	•	jobs always save
	•	invoices are correct
	•	files stay stored

Reliability builds confidence.

⸻

8️⃣ The Goal of the Next 3 Months

Your target should be:

10 businesses testing the app.

Not 100.

Not thousands.

Just 10 businesses using it regularly.

If even 3–5 businesses start depending on it, that’s a massive validation.

⸻

9️⃣ This Is When SaaS Actually Starts

Right now you’ve completed the hardest part technically.

But the next phase is actually harder:

product validation.

That’s when you learn:
	•	what users actually need
	•	what workflows matter
	•	what businesses will pay for

⸻

🔟 The Hidden Advantage You Have

You already built a complete workflow system.

Many startups take a year or more to reach that stage.

So now your biggest focus should be:

✔ stability
✔ user feedback
✔ trust

Not more code.

⸻

💡 One last piece of advice:
When you get your first real users, don’t ask them what features they want.

Instead ask:

“What part of your day does this save you time on?”

That question tells you whether your app is actually valuable.
