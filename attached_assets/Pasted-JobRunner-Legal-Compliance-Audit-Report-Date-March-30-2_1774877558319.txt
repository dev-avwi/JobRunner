JobRunner Legal & Compliance Audit
Report
Date: March 30, 2026 Auditor: Manus AI
Executive Summary
JobRunner’s legal foundation is surprisingly robust for an Early Access platform. The
documents correctly reference the Privacy Act 1988, the Australian Consumer Law
(ACL), and specifically address modern edge cases like GPS tracking, AI-generated
content, and SMS compliance.
However, because JobRunner operates as a B2B2C platform (handling data and
payments on behalf of other businesses), there are a few critical gaps related to thirdparty provider agreements (specifically Stripe Connect) and Australian
Telecommunications law that need to be closed to ensure you are fully protected from
liability.
Here is the detailed breakdown of what is solid, what is missing, and the exact text you
need to add to fix it.
1. Terms of Service Audit
What You Did Right
Australian Consumer Law (ACL): Section 20 perfectly handles the mandatory
ACL guarantees. You cannot contract out of these, and your phrasing correctly
acknowledges them while limiting liability elsewhere.
AI & SMS Disclaimers: Sections 24A and 24B correctly shift the liability for AI
miscommunications and Spam Act 2003 (Cth) compliance onto the tradie. This is
crucial.
Limitation of Liability: Capping liability to the amount paid in the preceding 12
months is standard SaaS best practice.
Critical Gaps to Fix
Gap 1: Stripe Connect Mandatory Boilerplate Because you are using Stripe Connect
(where tradies accept payments from clients), Stripe legally requires you to include
specific boilerplate text in your Terms of Service binding the tradie to the Stripe
Connected Account Agreement. Simply saying “Stripe’s terms apply” is not enough to
protect you if a tradie commits fraud.
The Fix: Add the following exact text to Section 10 (Payment Processing): >
“Payment processing services for users on JobRunner are provided by Stripe and
are subject to the Stripe Connected Account Agreement, which includes the
Stripe Terms of Service (collectively, the “Stripe Services Agreement”). By
agreeing to these terms or continuing to operate as a user on JobRunner, you
agree to be bound by the Stripe Services Agreement, as the same may be
modified by Stripe from time to time. As a condition of JobRunner enabling
payment processing services through Stripe, you agree to provide JobRunner
accurate and complete information about you and your business, and you
authorize JobRunner to share it and transaction information related to your use
of the payment processing services provided by Stripe.”
Gap 2: Call Recording Consent (Telecommunications Act) Under the
Telecommunications (Interception and Access) Act 1979 (Cth), recording or
transcribing phone calls without the knowledge of the caller is illegal. Your AI
Receptionist (Vapi.ai) transcribes these calls. Your Terms state that customers will be
informed it’s an “automated system,” but it must explicitly state that calls are
recorded/transcribed.
The Fix: Update Section 24A, bullet point 4 to read: > “You agree that the AI
Receptionist must play an automated greeting informing callers that they are
interacting with an AI system and that the call is being recorded and transcribed
for quality and business purposes, in compliance with Australian
telecommunications laws.”
2. Privacy Policy Audit
What You Did Right
GPS & Location Data: Section 5A is excellent. Explicitly stating the retention
period (12 months) and access controls protects you against surveillance
complaints.
Notifiable Data Breaches (NDB): Section 6A correctly references the OAIC and
the mandatory NDB scheme.
Employee Records Exemption: Section 7A correctly notes that the standard
Australian employee records exemption does not apply to your handling of the
tradies’ employees’ data. This is a common trap, and you avoided it.
Critical Gaps to Fix
Gap 1: Subcontractor Data The policy frequently mentions “employees” and “team
members,” but JobRunner also has a Subcontractor portal. Subcontractors are legally
distinct from employees in Australia.
The Fix: Update the “Information We Collect” and “Employee Records
Exemption” sections to explicitly include “subcontractors and independent
contractors.”
Gap 2: The “Controller vs. Processor” Distinction for Client Data JobRunner holds
data about the tradies’ clients (homeowners). Under privacy principles, the tradie is
the data “Controller” and JobRunner is the data “Processor.” If a homeowner emails
JobRunner asking to delete their data, JobRunner legally shouldn’t do it directly—you
must route that request to the tradie.
The Fix: Add a new section under “Your Rights” (Section 7): > “Data of Our
Customers’ Clients” > “JobRunner processes the personal information of our
customers’ clients (e.g., homeowners) solely on behalf of our customers. If you
are a client of a business that uses JobRunner and you wish to access, correct, or
delete your personal information, please contact that business directly. If we
receive such a request, we will forward it to the relevant business for them to
action.”
Gap 3: APP 5 Collection Notice at Signup While the Privacy Policy is comprehensive,
Australian Privacy Principle (APP) 5 requires you to notify users at the time of
collection (e.g., on the signup page).
The Fix: You don’t need to change the policy document, but on your /auth?
mode=signup page, right above the “Create Free Account” button, add a small
disclaimer: > “By creating an account, you agree to our [Terms of Service] and
acknowledge our [Privacy Policy].” (Make sure these are clickable links).
3. Support & Deletion Flows
Account Deletion: You have a /delete-account route, which complies with
Apple App Store and Google Play Store mandatory requirements (both stores
now require a clear, accessible way for users to delete their accounts and data
from within the app and via a web link).
Support Links: As previously verified, the footer and support pages now
correctly route to the legal documents.
Summary of Action Items
1. Update Terms of Service: Copy-paste the mandatory Stripe Connect boilerplate
into Section 10.
2. Update Terms of Service: Add the call recording consent requirement to the AI
Receptionist section.
3. Update Privacy Policy: Add the “Data of Our Customers’ Clients” clause to clarify
that homeowners must ask their tradie to delete their data, not you.
4. Update Signup UI: Ensure there is a small “By signing up you agree to our Terms
and Privacy Policy” text block with links on the registration form.
Once these four minor text additions are made, JobRunner’s legal framework will be
bulletproof for scaling in the Australian market.
