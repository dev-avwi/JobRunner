## The Customer Saga Framework (Core Concept)

The central idea: your client is the **hero** of their own story. You are the **mentor** (Yoda, not Luke). Every page of your website should tell their story — not yours.

The saga structure:

1. Hero has a desire
2. Encounters obstacles (the big bad problem)
3. Meets a mentor (you) who's already defeated the big bad
4. Gets a plan
5. Achieves the happy ending

This maps directly to your homepage layout:

- **Hero section** — flash forward to the happy ending (what they want)
- **Problem section** — the big bad and its symptoms
- **Solution section** — you as the mentor (empathy + authority)
- **Benefits** — 3D benefits (feature → benefit → deeper meaning)
- **Process plan** — 3 simple steps to work with you
- **Testimonials** — objection-crushing social proof
- **Features** — you vs. competition (or just your inclusions)
- **FAQs** — bust the final objections
- **CTA** — one clear action, repeated everywhere

## Module 1 — Customer Saga Research (What to Gather Before Writing)

### Ideal Client Avatar

Use **Perplexity AI** (not ChatGPT — it does real web research). Prompt:

> "I need help identifying my ideal client avatar. I run a [business type]. Here's what I know for sure: [list]. Here are my best guesses: [list]. Give me: demographics, a cleverly alliterated avatar name (e.g. Busy Ben), and detailed psychographics including goals, challenges, values, lifestyle, and how my service meets their needs."
> 

Tweak until it sounds like your actual clients.

### What Your Client Wants (The I Want Song)

- Narrow to **one single desire** based on your service focus
- Make it specific, not vague. Bad: "we give you more time." Good: "Packed calendar. Your dog's walk is covered."
- The desire fits one of: making/saving money, saving time/eliminating stress, health, status/social standing, or relationships
- **Their desire is almost never your service itself.** They don't want coaching. They want a happier life. They don't want dog training. They want a dog they can take anywhere.

### The Big Bad Problem

- One overarching problem, not many
- Must be instantly recognisable to your ideal client
- Comes with external symptoms (how it shows up daily) and internal emotions (how it makes them feel)
- Go deeper than "frustrated" — why are they frustrated? What does it make them doubt or regret?
- Value-add businesses (photo booth rental, event spaces): your "problem" is the gap between their current life and a more elevated experience. Lean less on pain, more on aspiration.

### You as the Mentor

Two components:

1. **Understanding** — show empathy. "I know how draining it is to be constantly pulled into IT emergencies when you should be driving your vision forward."
2. **Authority** — brief, stat-backed proof. Numbers hit both the logical and emotional brain. "I've helped 300 clients overcome obstacles" > "I have lots of experience."
3. **Philosophical statement** — what do you believe/stand for that your client would agree with? "I believe technology should empower growth without being a constant burden."

### Call to Action

- One direct CTA only (book a consultation, schedule a call)
- Never say "contact us" or "learn more" — passive language signals you don't believe in your own service
- If you had a cure for a disease, would you quietly ask people to contact you? Be bold.

### 3-Step Process Plan

- Step 1: your CTA (what they do right now)
- Step 2: everything you do for them (simplified into one sentence)
- Step 3: the happy after state
- Add a benefit to each step. Don't skip the numbered language — without it, it reads as three random features, not a reassuring process.

### Features vs Benefits (Critical)

- **Feature**: what something is (goal setting and accountability)
- **Benefit**: what it does for you (keeps you on track, increasing motivation)
- **Deeper meaning**: the real emotional payoff (you'll make meaningful strides toward your dreams)
- Formula: *"You'll get [feature] so you can [benefit] which means [deeper meaning]"*
- 80% of businesses stop at features. 20% get to benefits. Almost nobody goes to deeper meaning. That's your differentiation.

### Objections

- Top 3–5 objections, with a compelling rebuttal for each
- Price objection: anchor to more expensive alternatives, or flip it to "what's it costing you NOT to?"
- "Won't work for me" objection: show case studies of diverse clients, explain why it will work for their specific situation
- Trust objection: reinforce empathy + authority + testimonials + a human photo

### Negative Consequences + Success Vision

- People are 3x more motivated to avoid a bad outcome than to achieve a good one
- List 3 realistic negative consequences of not working with you
- List 3 ultimate successes they'll achieve
- Attach a specific positive emotion to each success (not just "happy" — use: proud, relieved, confident, empowered, focused)

### Deep Research (Perplexity)

- **Voice of customer messaging**: find exact phrases your clients use to describe their problems, desires, and past failures. Search real review sites only. Use these phrases in your copy.
- **Pre-purchase FAQs**: top 20 questions a prospect has before choosing a provider. Delete any that don't apply.
- **Industry statistics**: compelling numbers that create urgency. "Staged homes sell 88% faster and for 20% more."

## Module 2 — Writing Your Website Copy

### Tone Guidelines

- Avoid **woo woo copy**: vague, abstract, feel-good with no concrete meaning
- Avoid **corporate jargon**: synergy, innovative solutions, global paradigms — trust killers
- Aim for: clear, conversational, specific. Apple, Lyft, HubSpot all write this way on purpose.
- Tone scale 1–10: 1 = casual/quirky, 10 = polished-but-never-stuffy. Default is 5 for most businesses.

### Homepage Flow (in order)

1. Hero section (flash forward to the happy ending)
2. Problem section (big bad + symptoms)
3. Solution section (you as mentor: empathy + authority)
4. Benefits (3D: feature → benefit → deeper meaning)
5. Process plan (3 steps with benefits)
6. Testimonials (3 that crush objections or show results)
7. Features section (you vs. them, or just your inclusions)
8. FAQs (5 objection-busters)
9. Final CTA

### Hero Section Formulas

- Formula 1: *Stop [problem]. Start [desired outcome].* + sub-headline explaining how
- Formula 2: *[Unique benefit] for [target audience].* We deliver [key service] enabling you to [what matters most]
- Formula 3: *[Desired outcome] without [common hassle].* Our [service] provides [benefit] ensuring [result]
- Add: 3 two-to-three word positive outcomes ("Know yourself better", "Get a clear path forward", "Goals reached faster")
- Add social proof: how many clients helped, five-star graphic, key statistic

### About Page (Second Most Visited Page)

Six elements:

1. **Characters** — real humans, not corporate headshots
2. **Setting** — name your city, show you're a real business
3. **Conflict** — what was broken in your industry that you had to fix? Or your founder's passion story.
4. **Journey** — how did your approach develop? What's unique about how you do things?
5. **Resolution** — real outcomes for real clients. Evidence your approach works.
6. **Sequel** — where are you going? What bigger impact do you want to make?

> 82% of consumers want to buy from businesses whose values align with their own. Not their prices. Their values.
> 

### Testimonials (The Right Way)

- Ask 5 questions: What was your problem before? How did it make you feel? Describe working with me. How are things better now? How do you feel vs. before?
- Get them on a Zoom call — people honor appointments, they don't fill out forms
- Record the call, pull the best quotes, write the story in one short paragraph
- Never use "contact us and learn more" level testimonials. You need **result showcasers** (before/after with numbers) and **objection busters** (addresses a common doubt)
- Give each testimonial a 2–3 word headline. 5x more people read headlines than body text.
- Include a photo — grab from LinkedIn, ask permission
- 3 homepage testimonials max. Choose the bangers.

### Features vs. Comparison

- If your offer is genuinely better than competitors and you can prove it: use a comparison table (you vs. them)
- If you can't make a strong case: just list your features with benefits and deeper meanings
- Don't go overly negative on competition — it backfires and erodes trust

### Pricing Page (Always Include One)

- People expect it. If you don't have it, competitors who do will win the business.
- Pre-qualifies prospects so you stop wasting time on calls with people who can't afford you
- If you can't list hard prices: use starting-at prices, ranges, or tiers with variables explained
- Anchor pricing: show a higher-priced option to make your core offer feel attainable
- Never use defensive language that assumes people think your price is too high

### Pages You Actually Need

- Homepage, About, Pricing, FAQs, Success Stories (testimonials + case study)
- Service pages (one per main service, if multiple)
- Portfolio page (if visual service)
- **Do not add anything else.** Every page that doesn't funnel toward a sale works against you.

## Module 3 — Lead Magnet (The 96% Who Aren't Ready Yet)

### Why It Matters

96% of website visitors aren't ready to buy yet. A lead magnet captures them on your email list so you can coach the sale over time. Email is the only channel where **you own the list** — social platforms can disappear or kill your reach.

### What Works Now

- Checklist / cheat sheet — converts well, easy to create, actionable
- Resource list / swipe file — saves them research time
- 5–7 day challenge — builds bond over multiple touchpoints
- Template / blueprint — removes the blank page problem
- Short guide (5–20 pages) — modern ebook, focused on one result
- Interactive quiz — personalised output, natural email exchange

### What Doesn't Work Anymore

- Newsletters (nothing in it for them)
- Generic ebooks
- Anything emphasising what they'll **learn** instead of what they'll **get**

### Lead Magnet Mistakes

1. Promising learning instead of transformation. "10 makeup tips" < "10 secrets to turn back the clock 10 years"
2. Solving the wrong problem for your audience (career path quiz for executives — they're already on their path)
3. Boring title — power words, specific result, longer is fine
4. Not visually appealing — use Ideogram.ai for covers (handles text correctly unlike Midjourney)
5. Doesn't align with your paid offer — 100% overlap required

> Don't be afraid to give away your best stuff. The more you share freely, the more people want to pay. They'll either DIY it (they were never your client) or realise they need you to do it for them.
> 

### Email Sequence

- Delivery email: sends immediately, tone is warm and excited
- 5-email nurture sequence (one per day or every other day):
    - Day 1: quick easy-to-implement tip building on the lead magnet. Ask them to reply with their results.
    - Day 2: deeper dive into one key concept
    - Day 3: address a common sticking point
    - Day 4: more advanced strategy
    - Day 5: recap + first direct CTA in the PS line ("Ready for more help? Book a call.")
- Subject lines: curiosity-driven, benefit/result-focused, short and punchy
- Email provider recommendation: **Kit (formerly ConvertKit)** — free up to 10K subscribers, primary inbox delivery vs Mailchimp's promotions tab

## Module 4 — Building the Website (Hostinger)

### Color Rules

- Pick a palette with at least 1–2 neutral colors + 2 accent colors + 1 standout CTA color
- **CTA color rule**: use your standout color on CTA buttons ONLY. Nowhere else on the site. Not in text, not in the logo, not in icons. This is the most ignored rule that matters most.
- Avoid bold color blocking — most good sites use white space + great images + small pops of color
- Tool: **Palettmaker.com** — free, shows palettes in a web UI mockup

### Image Strategy

- **Happy customer photos** — show the happy after state, not the process ("sausage being made")
- Don't feature yourself in the hero section unless you're a known personal brand whose audience already recognises you
- Introduce yourself lower on the page (solution section) with a candid, friendly photo
- Vary: age, gender, ethnicity, scenario — lean toward your actual avatar
- Tool: **Midjourney** — best for generating cohesive sets of happy customer photos. Use `--style raw` for more natural look. Specify activities not poses ("playing with kids on the beach" > "smiling couple")
- For lead magnet covers: **Ideogram.ai** — handles text on images correctly

### Section Advice

- Never skip the step numbers in your process plan ("Step 1, Step 2, Step 3") — without them it reads as random features
- Bold key phrases throughout — people skim, they don't read. If they only take one thing from each paragraph, bold it.
- Don't include social media icons in the header or mid-page. Footer only. You don't want to send warmed-up visitors back to Instagram.
- Booking page: keep it stripped down. One job. No images, no color scheme distractions. Tell them exactly what happens after they submit.
- Form fields: ask only what you need. Every extra field = friction = fewer completions.

# 🎥 Video 2 — Building an Animated Website with Claude Code + Figma + Hailuo/Kling

**Channel:** (web dev creator) | **Focus:** Design-to-code workflow with animated AI characters

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F2OpUJsf68g

## What Was Built

A fully animated hero section website built from a Figma design using Claude Code. Features: animated Pixar-style characters generated with Hailuo Nano Banana 2, animated with Kling/Cedanse 2.0, dropped into the site as videos. Grid SVG background with radial vignette mask. Word-by-word H1 animation. Fully responsive with mobile burger nav. Pushed to GitHub and hosted on Cloudflare in ~5 minutes.

## The Workflow (Step by Step)

### Step 1 — Figma to Claude Code

Two options:

- **If you have Figma Pro**: use Dev Mode → MCP → copy example prompt directly
- **If you don't**: screenshot the design → drag into Claude → prompt: *"Make this into a full screen hero section website. Font is [font name]. H1 is [size]px."*
- Model: **Claude Opus 4.6** for the initial build

### Step 2 — Terminal for Micro-Adjustments

Once initial build is done in Claude desktop app, switch to terminal:

- Prompt specific fixes: "H1 should be regular and semi-bold weight", "star icon at end of text not showing correctly"
- Have the website open on the left, Claude terminal on the right
- Refresh browser after each change

### Step 3 — Add SVG Assets

- Export SVGs from Figma (logo, icons, background elements)
- Create an `/images` folder in the project
- Prompt Claude: "Add grid.svg that's in the images folder to the full screen background. It should sit behind everything filling the entire page."
- For vignette: "Add a radial mask to this to create a vignette. Tighten it up."

### Step 4 — Generate Characters (Hailuo Nano Banana 2)

- Use **Hailuo Nano Banana 2** (or Pro) for image generation
- Prompt for Pixar-style characters on white background
- Iterate — first results may be too on-the-nose (too close to existing IP). Adjust until original.
- Export as PNGs, add to `/images` folder
- Prompt Claude: "I want to add two character PNG images into the design. They're in the images folder. Position them like this." (attach screenshot of intended layout)

### Step 5 — Animate Characters (Kling/Cedanse 2.0)

- Use **Kling Cedanse 2.0** for character animation (available via Hailuo)
- Why Cedanse 2.0: super precise, static camera, no unwanted panning or cropping. Essential for characters on a website.
- Prompt: "This character is on a solid white background. He's looking around. Static camera positioning, no panning."
- Download the video files

### Step 6 — Remove Background (Optional, DaVinci Resolve)

- Drag video into DaVinci Resolve
- Use Magic Mask, draw around the character
- Add solid white background layer
- Export clean video
- Alternative in-code solution: prompt Claude to add `blend-mode: darken` on the videos — this punches out the white, effectively making it transparent without DaVinci

### Step 7 — Compress Videos

- 6MB video files are too heavy for a hero section
- Prompt Claude in terminal: "Can we compress the videos?"
- Result: 6MB → 650KB. Quality loss is minimal, performance gain is massive.

### Step 8 — Add Animations to Page Elements

- Prompt: "Have the grid come on screen first. Grow in the H1 appearing one word at a time."
- Claude handles the animation logic from a plain English instruction

### Step 9 — Make Responsive

- Prompt: "Make it responsive. Nav links should turn into a burger icon. Show a full screen nav popup with a cross at the top. Hide the characters on mobile. Have the login button full width."
- One prompt handles the entire mobile breakpoint

### Step 10 — Deploy

- Prompt: "Let's push this to a GitHub repo, then show me the steps of publishing this live on Cloudflare."
- Total deploy time: ~5 minutes
- Cloudflare hosting is free in most cases. Connect a domain and you're live.

## Key Tips

- **`blend-mode: darken`** — easiest way to remove white backgrounds from videos in-browser without DaVinci. One line of CSS, ask Claude to apply it.
- **Cedanse 2.0 over other video models for UI** — other models pan, zoom, and reframe. For characters sitting inside a website, you need static camera. Cedanse 2.0 respects this.
- **Compress in-terminal** — don't forget to ask Claude to compress video assets before shipping. The compression is dramatic and Claude handles it with a single prompt.
- **Figma Dev Mode MCP** — if you're on Figma Pro, this is a significant upgrade over screenshotting. Claude gets exact values, not guesses.
- **Word-by-word H1 animation** — one of the best low-effort high-impact animations for hero sections. Ask Claude directly for it.

## Apply to Your Projects

- **Any app landing page** — this workflow (screenshot → Claude → terminal for refinement → deploy on Cloudflare) can produce a professional landing page in under an hour
- **Kindred** — an animated, character-driven hero section would fit the warmth of the brand. Pixar-style characters with the blend-mode trick is a genuinely unique approach for an emotion-driven app
- **Criti** — a design-forward landing page built with this exact process would be a credibility signal to designer/dev users. Show that your landing page itself was built with care.
- **`blend-mode: darken` trick** — useful beyond just character videos. Any time you want a white-background PNG or video to feel transparent on a coloured background.
- **GitHub + Cloudflare** — if you're not already hosting static sites this way, it's worth switching. Free, fast, and Claude can walk you through the entire deploy process in terminal.

# 🎥 Video 3 — Build a $10,000 Website with Claude Code

**Channel:** (web dev creator) | **Focus:** Full premium website build workflow, skills, motion, deploy

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VMvZuhcDdnw

## What Was Built

A premium restaurant website (dark moody steakhouse) built from scratch using Claude Code desktop app. Scroll-driven hero video, cinematic motion, cursor interactions, per-section micro-animations. Deployed to a custom domain via Hostinger. Total cost: ~$60 (Claude Pro + hosting + domain).

## The $10,000 Website Checklist

Eight things that separate a $10K site from a $200 one — grouped into three categories:

**Taste**: Point of view, Typography, Colour

**Substance**: Hierarchy, Imagery

**Felt quality**: Motion, Mobile (designed, not just shrunk), Invisible stuff (fast, finished feel)

## Setup

- Claude Code desktop app, Code mode (not Chat mode)
- Create a dedicated project folder, connect it in the app
- Switch to **auto mode** (bottom left) — lets Claude work without stopping to ask permission at every step

## Installing Skills (The Key Differentiator)

Out of the box, Claude defaults to the same overused fonts, predictable layouts, and generic AI output. Skills fix that.

### Skill 1 — Front End Design (Anthropic-built)

- Paste the skill link into Claude with: `"Install this skill"`
- Install globally
- Runs silently in the background
- **What it does**: bans the most overused fonts on the internet, pushes Claude toward bold design direction instead of generic AI output, trains Claude on restrained sensory copywriting

### Skill 2 — UI UX Pro Max (community-built)

- Paste the skill link into Claude with: `"Install this plugin using NPM"` (exact phrasing matters)
- Install globally
- Ships with 57 UI styles, 95 colour palettes, 56 font pairings
- You call it directly at the start of a build: `/ui-ux-pro-max` at the top of your prompt

## The Build Prompt (The Whole Video in One Pattern)

```
/ui-ux-pro-max
[brief description of what you're building]
Ask me clarifying questions.
```

That last line — **"Ask me clarifying questions"** — is what makes this work. It forces Claude to stop and think before building, and it comes back with style direction options to choose from (e.g. dark moody luxury vs. Pacific Northwest modern vs. classic chophouse). Your answers become the entire site. The more specific you are at this stage, the less you'll have to fight Claude on revisions.

## References Before You Prompt

- **Dribbble** — designers posting work, every style
- **Awwwards** — award-winning sites, highest quality bar
- **Pinterest** — search "modern website design [your niche]"
- Take 3–5 screenshots of sites you like. You're not copying — you're showing Claude your taste.
- Describing visual taste in words is hard. Screenshots are your shortcut.

## Imagery Strategy

Claude can't solve imagery on its own. Three tiers:

1. **Custom photography** — best, drop into project folder and tell Claude what to do with it
2. **AI image → AI video** — use ChatGPT image model for stills, then Google Veo 3.1 or similar to animate into video. Aggregator tools (like ElevenLabs) give you image + video + upscale in one tab.
3. **Ask Claude to write the image prompt** — Claude already knows your project, so ask it: "Write me an image generation prompt for the hero asset that matches our brand and design."

## Pre-made Components (21st.dev)

- Browse **21st.dev** for free pre-built web components, animations, scroll effects, buttons
- Two uses: browse for inspiration, or copy a prompt that tells Claude exactly how to recreate a specific component
- **Important**: Claude will push back if a component requires React and your project is a simple static site. That's correct behaviour — Claude protects your architecture. Don't override it.
- If Claude says no to something: explain your constraint and ask it to rebuild the effect without changing the project architecture

## Debugging Pattern

When something breaks:

1. Tell Claude what went wrong and what you expected instead
2. If it doesn't fix it, tell Claude to dig deeper
3. Claude can open your page in a browser, inspect it live, and find the real problem
4. Keep pushing — most issues resolve within 2–3 attempts if you give specific feedback

## The Polish Pass (Chapter 7 — Most Important)

### Step 1 — Grade yourself against the checklist

Paste the 8-item checklist into Claude and ask: "Where does this site land against each of these criteria? Be honest."

You'll get a split: some strong, some mixed. That's normal. Now you know exactly what's left.

### Step 2 — Batch fix prompt

Don't fix items one at a time. Ask Claude to **propose a batch of fixes** and lead with intent, not specifics:

> "We need more handcrafted micro interactions. The lower sections feel a bit generic. We don't need to make them busier, just more expensive."
> 

Note what's happening: you're telling Claude how you want it to **feel**, not what to add. Claude is better at translating intent into specifics than most people are at writing specs. Fewer tokens, more cohesive result.

### Step 3 — Section-by-section cursor/motion pass

Scroll through every section. Find the ones that still feel flat. For each:

- Give Claude **one specific motion or cursor interaction to add** — one per section
- Keep all of them restrained and subtle
- After Claude implements: push back with "make it more subtle, more refined" until it feels alive without being noisy

Examples from the build:

- Reserve a Table section: ember glow → "Make the embers move like fire. Have them react to the cursor."
- Story section: "Add elegant micro movements and cursor interactions here. This section is very static." → candlelight halo that follows the cursor. First attempt too obvious → "Make it more subtle, more refined." → trailing motion that lags behind the cursor. Ship that version.

> "That layer is the difference between a site that looks expensive and one that **feels** expensive."
> 

## Copy Quality

- The Front End Design skill trains Claude toward restrained sensory writing
- Example: "Six dishes, one fire." — no adjectives, maximum impact
- **The Inter font problem**: Inter is so overused on AI sites it instantly signals "AI made this." If you see Inter, ask Claude to swap it. Good alternative: Geist.

## Mobile Pass

- Claude makes sites responsive by default, but responsive ≠ designed for phones
- Ask Claude explicitly: "Do a dedicated pass on the mobile version. Think about what should be hidden on small screens, what should be tighter, and what should resize."

## Deployment (Hostinger)

- **Simple static site** (single HTML file, no login/checkout/database): Premium plan
- **Complex site** (user accounts, payment, backend): Business plan (supports Node.js)
- Free domain included on 12-month+ plans
- **Critical zip tip**: don't zip the project folder itself — zip the **files inside** the folder. If you zip the whole folder, Hostinger nests your files one level too deep and your domain loads blank.
    - Mac: select all inside folder → right click → Compress
    - Windows: select all → right click → Send To → Compressed Folder
    - Result: `index.html` sits at the top level of the zip

## Apply to Your Projects

- **Install both skills now** — Front End Design + UI UX Pro Max. Install globally, they work for every project.
- **The `/ui-ux-pro-max` + clarifying questions pattern** is the best starting prompt for any web project
- **The Inter swap** — check all your existing Claude Code web projects. If Inter is anywhere, swap it.
- **The batch intent prompt** — use this pattern when a section feels flat: describe how it should feel, not what to add
- **The cursor/motion pass** — this is what turns a professional site into an expensive one. One subtle interaction per flat section.
- **Deployment zip tip** — this catches people every time. Save this note.

# 🎥 Video 7 — Clone an Award-Winning Website in 10 Minutes: The AI Architect Workflow (Samir)

## What Was Built

A clone of the Jesko Jets award-winning website (Awwwards website of the year). Cinematic cloud-push hero scroll, plane morphing from photorealistic to wireframe, seamless rotating globe loop. Built in ~10 minutes, $0 cost.

## The Core Insight

Stop trying to get one AI model to do everything. Instead: **orchestrate a team of specialised AI tools**, each handling what it does best. The workflow has six phases.

---

## Phase 1 — The Architect's Deconstruction

Before building anything, identify the 3–5 "impossible components" that make the site feel premium. For Jesko Jets:

1. **Hero scroll** — camera pushes through a window into clouds, speed tied to scroll position
2. **Plane morph** — photorealistic jet transitions to a CAD wireframe as you scroll
3. **Globe loop** — seamless infinite rotation with zero stutter or jump

Rule: don't build the whole site at once. **Generate the components first**, then build the website around them.

---

## Phase 2 — Generative Assets (Google Whisk + Google Flow)

**Google Whisk** = image generation using reference images as style/subject inputs

**Google Flow** = frame-to-frame video generation (give it a start frame and end frame, it generates the motion between them)

### Hero scroll asset

1. Screenshot the hero section of the original site
2. Paste into Whisk as a **subject** → prompt for the start frame (inside the cabin looking out)
3. Refine: "remove the [brand] text"
4. Screenshot a further-in moment of the original → give to Whisk as a new subject → prompt for the end frame (camera has passed through the window)
5. Download both frames
6. In Google Flow → change type to **Frames to Video** → upload start frame + end frame → generate
7. Pick the best output

### Plane morph asset

1. Screenshot the photorealistic plane from the original site → Whisk subject
2. Prompt: generate a clean photorealistic plane
3. Refine to remove text
4. Take that generated plane → give as Whisk subject
5. Screenshot the wireframe from the original site → also give as Whisk subject
6. Prompt: generate the wireframe version of the same plane
7. Download both (photorealistic + wireframe)
8. Google Flow → Frames to Video → upload both → generate

### Globe asset

1. Screenshot the globe from the original site → Whisk subject
2. Prompt: seamless rotating globe
3. Refine to remove text
4. Click **Animate** directly inside Whisk (no need to go to Flow for this one)
5. Give a clear motion prompt: smooth seamless rotation, no jump
6. Download

## Phase 3 — Optimising for the Web (Ezgif Frame Extraction)

**Critical step most people skip.** You can't put video files directly into a scroll-controlled website — they're too heavy and you can't control playback with the scrollbar.

**Solution**: convert scroll-controlled videos to individual image frame sequences.

Tool: **Ezgif** (free, browser-based) — search "video to frames ezgif"

Settings: 15 fps (sweet spot — smooth enough for web, not too heavy. 30fps = overkill)

Output: hundreds of individual JPEG frames in a zip file

**Three different treatments for three different asset types:**

| Asset | Treatment | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hero scroll | Frame sequence (15fps JPEGs) | Scroll position scrubs through frames |
| Plane morph | Frame sequence (15fps JPEGs) | Same — scroll-driven |
| Globe | Use video file as-is | Continuous background loop — just set to loop forever |

Name your extracted folders clearly: `sequence1`, `sequence2`. Place them in the `/public` directory of your project before building.

- Screen recording of the original website (for design, UX, and aesthetic reference)

**What it outputs:**

- React component structure

- Complete copy
- Every implementation instruction the coding agent will need

> "This document is now the brain of our project."
> 

Paste the entire blueprint into the coding agent as the first message. It follows it exactly.

---

## Phase 5 — The Build (Antigravity / Claude Code)

- Drag all asset folders (hero sequence, plane sequence, globe video) into the `/public` directory **before** writing any code
- Paste the entire Gemini blueprint into Antigravity's chat → hit enter
- It follows the blueprint: canvas component for hero, CSS triggers for the plane morph, video player for the globe
- Minor linter errors → paste errors back → auto-fixed
- After the base build: ask "what other features could we add?" → it suggests sound design, custom cursor, micro-interactions → tell it to implement them

## Phase 6 — Autonomous QA (TestSprite)

**TestSprite** = an autonomous QA agent that runs as an MCP server inside your editor. It has "eyes" — it can actually see and interact with the running website.

Setup: configure TestSprite as an MCP server in your editor (Claude Code / Antigravity)

Workflow:

1. Give it a simple test command in the chat
2. It generates test cases from your PRD and code
3. Runs all test cases **in parallel** (13 cases tested simultaneously in the demo)
4. Outputs a test report file with pass/fail for each case
5. For failed cases: shows a video recording of the failure, the error, the steps, and the suggested fix code
6. Paste the fix report back into Antigravity → it resolves everything

Result: production-quality QA without manually testing on different devices and network conditions.

## Honest Limitations

These are **static animated sites** — no CMS, no blog, no e-commerce, no user login. Built for service businesses that need a premium front door, not SaaS companies or online stores.

## Apply to Your Projects

- **The two-image scroll technique** — assembled + exploded product is the formula. Any physical product (hardware, packaging, device) can use this. For app landing pages, you could use an app UI "assembled" vs. individual feature screens "exploded."
- **Nano Banana 2 at 2K resolution** — always specify the resolution. This applies to any product photography prompt, not just for this technique.
- **Kling start frame + end frame** — this is the most controlled video generation workflow in the notes. You specify both endpoints exactly. Use this any time you need a precise camera motion or product transition.
- **Three.js scroll scrubbing** — the mechanism (video plays forward/backward based on scroll position) is one of the highest-impact landing page effects possible. Claude Code builds the whole thing from a plain English prompt in minutes.
