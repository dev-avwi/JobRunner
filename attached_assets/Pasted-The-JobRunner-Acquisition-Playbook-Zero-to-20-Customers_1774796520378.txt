The JobRunner Acquisition Playbook:
Zero to 20 Customers
Prepared by: Manus AI Date: March 29, 2026
Getting the first 20 paying customers is the hardest part of building any B2B SaaS. You
cannot rely on SEO, paid ads, or organic virality at this stage. Your first 20 customers
will come from hand-to-hand combat—direct outreach, personal networks, and
solving acute, immediate pain points for individual business owners [1].
This playbook provides a step-by-step strategy tailored specifically for the Australian
trades market.
1. The Core Strategy: Why Tradies Switch
Australian tradies are generally time-poor and hesitant to adopt new technology
unless the immediate benefit significantly outweighs the learning curve [2]. They do
not switch software because of “better UI” or “cleaner code.”
They switch because of acute pain:
Lost Revenue: Forgetting to invoice for a job or losing track of a quote.
Wasted Time: Spending hours every night doing admin instead of being with
family [3].
Complexity: Existing software (like ServiceM8 or Tradify) becoming too bloated,
expensive, or difficult for their subcontractors to use [4].
The JobRunner Wedge
Your competitive advantage is not just price; it is the AI Receptionist and the Free
Tier. No other platform offers a dedicated Australian phone number with an AI that
answers calls
24
⁄7 and books jobs directly into the system. This is a massive hook.
2. The 30-Day Execution Plan
Week 1: Fix the Blockers & Tap Your Immediate Network
Before you pitch a single stranger, you must ensure the product is ready for a real
business to rely on it.
Action Items:
1. Publish the Mobile Apps: As identified in the technical audit, the App Store and
Google Play links currently 404. Tradies live on their phones; without a mobile
app, your conversion rate will be near zero.
2. The “Friends and Family” Launch: Find 3-5 tradies in your immediate network
(friends, family, or tradespeople who have recently worked on your house).
3. The Pitch: Do not ask them to buy. Ask them to test. > “Hey [Name], I’ve spent
the last year building a new app to help tradies manage jobs and get rid of nighttime admin. I’m looking for 5 brutal, honest testers to try it out for free and tell
me what sucks. Can I buy you a beer/coffee and show you how it works?”
Week 2: Community-Led Growth (Facebook Groups)
Australian tradies are highly active in specific Facebook groups. However, these groups
are aggressively moderated against spam and self-promotion [5]. You must lead with
value, not a sales pitch.
Target Groups:
Tradies in Australia (Large, active community)
Sydney Local Tradies & Small Business (Or your local equivalent)
Small Business Support Group Australia
The “Trojan Horse” Strategy: Do not post a link to JobRunner. Instead, post a highly
relatable question or a free resource.
Post Template 1 (The Pain Point):
“Question for the solo operators here: How many hours a week are you losing to
quoting and invoicing at night? I’m an Aussie dev building a simpler alternative to
Tradify/ServiceM8 because my mates were complaining about the cost. What’s the
one feature you wish your current software actually did well?”
Post Template 2 (The Free Value):
“Hey everyone, I noticed a lot of guys struggling with missed calls while on the
tools. I built an AI receptionist tool that answers calls with an Aussie accent and
texts you the lead details. I’m looking for 3 businesses to test it for free this month.
Drop a comment if you want to try it.”
When people comment, move to Direct Messages (DMs). Set up a 15-minute Zoom or
phone call.
Week 3: Highly Targeted Direct Outreach
Once you have 3-5 beta users from your network and Facebook, start direct outbound.
The Target: Look for small trade businesses (1-5 staff) on Google Maps or local
directories that have good reviews but terrible websites (indicating they are busy but
lack modern tech).
The Channel: SMS or WhatsApp. Tradies rarely check cold emails.
The Script (Keep it under 3 sentences):
“Hey [Name], saw your great reviews on Google. I’m a local Aussie dev who just built
JobRunner—a simpler, cheaper alternative to ServiceM8. I’m giving lifetime free
access to my first 10 users in exchange for feedback. Worth a 5-min chat?”
Week 4: The “Do It For Them” Onboarding
When a tradie says “yes” to a trial, do not just send them a link and expect them to
set it up. They will not do it.
You must offer White-Glove Onboarding:
1. Ask them to export their client list from Xero/MYOB or their current software.
2. You import the data into JobRunner for them.
3. You set up their logo and quote templates.
4. Hand them an account that is 100% ready to use on day one.
This removes the single biggest barrier to software adoption: the setup friction [6].
3. Pricing Strategy for the First 20
Your goal right now is not to maximize revenue; it is to maximize usage and
testimonials.
1. Customers 1-5 (The Beta Cohort): Free for life. In exchange, they must agree to
a 15-minute feedback call every two weeks, and provide a video testimonial once
they love the product.
2. Customers 6-20 (The Early Adopters): Offer them the Pro Plan at a heavily
discounted rate (e.g., 39/month).
3. The AI Upsell: For any customer, offer the Vapi AI Receptionist at cost (e.g.,
$30/month) just to get them hooked on the magic of the feature.
4. Key Metrics to Track
Do not track website visitors or page views. At this stage, only three numbers matter:
1. Conversations Started: How many tradies did you speak to this week? (Target:
20/week)
2. Demos Booked: How many agreed to let you show them the app? (Target:
5/week)
3. Active Workspaces: How many accounts actually created a job or sent an invoice
this week? (Target: 2 new active accounts/week)
Summary
Winning your first 20 B2B customers requires doing things that do not scale [1]. You
must be the sales rep, the onboarding specialist, and the customer support team. By
leveraging the AI Receptionist as a unique hook, targeting active Facebook
communities, and doing the heavy lifting of data migration for your early users, you
can bypass the noise and establish a solid foundational user base.
