# 🎥 Video 1 — Mike: $0 to $500K/Month Web Design Agency

**Channel:** Pavlo | **Guest:** Mike (GoHighLevel SaaS agency, contractor niche)

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TjELwAwigXc&list=LL&index=8&t=1s

## Who Mike Is

24-year-old, originally a software engineer. Started with a $997 course from Iman Gadzhi, spent 6 months earning $200 total. Pivoted to the GoHighLevel website model after finding it in a community. Got 17 clients in 10 days. Partnered with Kai Stone (marketing), went from $45K/month combined to $500K/month in one year. Currently 1,300+ clients, 7 closers, 3 setters, ~$150K/month in ad spend, ~40% margins.

## The Business Model

**Core offer:** Website + automations for contractors (plumbers, electricians, roofers etc.) for $297/month

- Website (GoHighLevel hosted, now migrating to custom-coded)
- Missed-call text back (auto-texts missed callers)
- Lead follow-up (contact form submissions trigger automated follow-up)
- Google review campaign + funnel link (filters bad reviews before they post)

**Upsells:**

- Google My Business optimisation — $500 one-time
- Google Local Service Ads management — $500/month
- SEO — $500/month
- Quarterly pricing discount: $800/month → $500/month if they pay 3 months upfront

**Why it works:**

- Low barrier to entry ($297 feels like nothing to a contractor doing $5K+ jobs)
- They've been burned by $1,500/month ad agencies before — this feels safe to try
- Once they're a client and happy, upselling is easy — credit card already on file, just need permission
- Recurring revenue: clients just keep paying. "I thought everyone would cancel after a month. They didn't."

## How He Got Clients (Cold Outreach Playbook)

### Step 1 — Build the list

- Go to Google Maps, search contractor types (plumbers, electricians, roofers, landscapers)
- Filter: must have a phone number. Later refined to: minimum 5 Google reviews, Google Business Profile verified
- Zero reviews = bad client. Unverified GBP = nightmare to work with
- Used Outscraper to pull lists at scale

### Step 2 — Cold SMS first, then call

- Send a simple cold SMS → anyone who responds positively → call them
- They're already warm → higher show rate → higher close rate
- Much easier than cold calling everyone blind

### Step 3 — The "Julie Becker" cold call script

> "Hey, is this [John's Plumbing]? Yeah — you guys did a job for a friend of my mom's, Julie Becker. I don't know if that rings a bell... Yeah, well, it was a long time ago. But she mentioned you guys didn't have a website or anything, so I figured I'd just build you one. Want to check it out?"
> 
- Julie Becker doesn't exist. It's a pattern interrupt that gets past the "not interested" reflex
- Any follow-up questions: "I don't know, it's a friend of my mom's"
- Result: they say "sure I guess" → you have a meeting
- Mike got this from Kai. His results tripled after switching to it

### Volume Mike was doing at peak

- **400–700 dials per day**
- Booking 30–40 appointments per day
- ~20% show rate → 5–10 meetings → 2–3 closes
- ~10% close rate on everyone who books = $450+ MRR per day added

## The Partnership Model (Mike + Kai)

**Why it worked:**

- Both were already independently successful (Mike at $15–18K/month, Kai at $30K/month) before partnering
- Complementary skills: Kai = marketing/ads/sales, Mike = operations/systems/hiring/training
- Neither needed the other to survive → negotiated from strength, not desperation
- Partnered because they could go further together, not because either was failing

**Mike's advice on partnerships:**

- Don't partner from the start if you're just getting going
- Learn sales first — if your partner gets sick and you can't sell, you're stuck. You can't train closers on a skill you don't have
- Build separately until you can see each other's skills clearly, then decide
- "If you're a technical person and a weirdo (which, if you're being honest, you probably are) — learn to be social first. Why would I partner with you if I don't like you?"

## Scaling Systems (What Mike Built After Partnering)

1. **Hired closers, setters, CSMs** — structured sales process so results didn't depend on Kai's charisma
2. **Deep onboarding/training** — for every hire. "Are you giving your people the tools they need to succeed? Or are you blaming them for not knowing what you already know?"
3. **Accountability structure** — one CSM responsible for each client A-to-Z. No blaming between departments. "It's on you" is clearer than "it's the VA's fault"
4. **CSMs should have sales skills** — not just technical. Contractors will run over meek CSMs. You need people who can upsell and hold their ground
5. **AI replacing VAs** — currently replacing service delivery VAs with AI automation. Not recommending this for anyone starting out
6. **Switching from GHL to custom-coded sites** — at 1,300 clients, GHL has limitations. Building a system where CSMs can type plain English to update a client's site

## Revenue & Profit Numbers

- $297/month base × 1,300+ clients = ~$390K+ MRR from base
- Upsells bring total to $500K+/month
- ~40% margins currently ($150K/month in ads is biggest cost)
- Projecting 50–60% margins as ads improve and AI reduces headcount
- Cutting 30% of payroll by replacing with AI over next few weeks

## Key Mindset Points

**On saturation:**

> "You have no idea what saturated means. To get to 10 mil a month at $300/client = 33,000 clients. There are tens of millions of contractors in the US. I'd have less than 1% of the market and be at $120M a year and a $500M exit. How is that saturated?"
> 

**On overanalysis:**

- Biggest mistake: overanalysing before you have a client
- You don't need a logo, website, team, Facebook ads knowledge, or a niche strategy
- You need: Stripe, a list of leads, a dialer, and to start calling
- "Stop thinking. Just call people. You're going to be bad at first. That's the point."

**On the "lifestyle business" sweet spot:**

- $50–100K/month with 3 employees (appointment setter, closer, CSM) = ~60–70% margins
- At that scale: $30–40K/month take-home. "Both my life and your life can be lived very comfortably on $20K/month."
- Move to a cheaper country: same $10K/month = private chef, trainer, assistant, maid in Colombia, Thailand, or Bali

**On sales as the #1 skill:**

> "If you can sell, that's all you need. You can always get a job. You can always make friends. You can always make business relationships. Sales is the most transferable skill in existence."
> 

## Apply to Your Situation

- **This model isn't directly your path** — you're building products, not an agency. But the *principles* are transferable
- **Cold outreach pattern** — if you need to validate JobRunner or Criti, the cold SMS → call warm leads playbook is directly usable. Cold DM on LinkedIn → warm anyone who responds → get on a call
- **"Julie Becker" pattern** — a pattern interrupt that gets past the "not interested" reflex works in any cold outreach context. The principle: reference something specific to create familiarity before making your ask
- **Volume as a skill builder** — Mike did 400–700 dials/day not just for clients but to build sales skills through sheer reps. If you ever need to do user interviews or cold outreach for validation, go high volume early — you get better fast
- **Partnership timing** — if you ever consider a co-founder or partner for one of your apps, Mike's framework is the right one: both people should be independently successful first, complementary skills, mutual respect from earned credibility
- **AI replacing ops** — at $500K/month he's cutting 30% of his team with AI. This is the direction everything is going. The apps you're building are part of that wave — the opportunity is in building the tools that replace the VAs, not in being the VA
- **The lifestyle number** — useful to have in mind. What's your target MRR where you feel secure and have your time back? Build toward that specific number, not "as much as possible"

# 🎥 Video 2 — How to Sell AI Websites as a Business (Full System)

**Channel:** Daryl Wilson | **Focus:** Lead gen, outreach, AI website creation, Stripe billing, ongoing management

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bqhvsKS7G9k

## What This Covers

A complete system for turning AI website building into a recurring revenue business. Five steps from zero to paying clients.

---

## Step 1 — Planning: Who to Target

**Best niches for AI web design (ranked by opportunity):**

- Contractors (plumbers, electricians, roofers, builders) — almost always have terrible websites, make good money, no problem paying
- Property management / real estate agents — simple sites, just need good presentation and lead capture
- Insurance advisors — want leads, clean presentation, easy to build for

**Why local beats everywhere:**

- Local clients = phone calls are possible = higher close rate
- You can say "I'm a local in [city]" — instantly more credible than a random cold email
- Focus on your local community first. Spread out once you have momentum.

**Filter for leads:**

- Target businesses with visibly bad websites (no CTA, broken elements, 2010-era design)
- Skip businesses with decent sites — waste of time
- Use Google Maps: search "[niche] + [city]", click through each result, check their website

---

## Step 2 — Lead Generation & Outreach

### Building your lead list

- Open Google Sheets with columns: business name, contact name, email, phone, address, message, follow-up (yes/no)
- For each prospect: note what happened on every contact attempt
- Mark warm leads for follow-up, cold responses as no

### Finding leads faster (two automation routes)

1. **Upwork** — hire a VA for lead generation or cold calling on your behalf. They build the list and do the outreach.
2. **N8N automation** — Google Maps lead scraper that pulls all contact info to a spreadsheet automatically (mentioned as a course topic in his community)

### Outreach priority order

1. **Phone call first** — people trust voices over cold emails. Much higher close rate. Say: *"Hey, I saw you guys down the road. I'm a web designer in the community. I thought you might need a website."*
2. **Email via contact form** — if no phone or they don't answer. Use a short, personalised template.
3. **SMS text** — use the SMS template for a quick informal reach-out

### Email template structure (first outreach)

- Introduce yourself, mention you're local
- Briefly say you help small businesses
- Ask if they have a few minutes
- No portfolio link needed on the first touch
- Sign off with: name, city, phone, website

### After someone responds

- Reply with: "Here's a quick example of your new website" (attach the preview you built with their logo)
- Ask: their email, logo file, a few photos, business information
- Keep it brief — they're not asking tons of questions. Your job is to sell the vision.

## Step 3 — AI Website Creation (Hostinger Horizons)

### Why Hostinger Horizons over other tools

- All-in-one: AI website builder + hosting + domain + monthly reports + name server management
- Can host up to 50 websites on the Business plan (relevant if you're managing multiple clients)
- Generates AI websites from prompts directly

### The prompt workflow

1. **Warm up ChatGPT** first: *"I want to create a website using Hostinger Horizons. Do they have any tips to make a creative and responsive website?"* — this loads the context so ChatGPT understands the platform
2. **Generate the full prompt**: *"Based on this information, create me a modern professional [niche] website for clients in [city]. Use colors: [palette]. Make it fully optimised, SEO-friendly, and professional."*
3. Copy the output, remove anything that doesn't apply to the client, paste into Hostinger Horizons → Generate
4. Optional shortcut: ask ChatGPT to summarise the full prompt into 2–3 paragraphs if the first version feels too broad

### Making it feel like their website (the closer move)

- Download their existing logo
- Upload it into Horizons: *"Add this logo throughout the entire website including the header and footer"*
- When they see their own logo on the new site, they're far more likely to say yes

### Post-generation refinements via prompt

- *"Make the menu smaller. Too many pages. Make them a drop-down menu."*
- *"Make the buttons visible. They should match the design style."*
- Use **Edit Content** mode in Horizons to manually update text and images without prompting

### Backend setup (required for contact forms to work)

- Go to Integrations → connect a **Supabase** account
- Create a new Supabase project, link it to the Horizons site
- Without this, form submissions won't save anywhere

### Publish and send

- Click Publish → View Site → copy the live URL
- Email the client the live preview with their logo

## Step 4 — Closing the Deal (Stripe)

### Pricing tiers

| Tier | Setup fee | Monthly |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic starter | $150–250 | $25–35 |
| Standard | $299–600 | $40–60 |
| Premium | $600–1,000 | $75–120 |

**Recommendation**: start at Standard ($299 setup + $39/month). Clients making $8–15K/month don't blink at $39/month.

### Stripe setup

- Free to use — no monthly fee, no credit check, just a bank account
- Create a **Product**: "Web Design Services" with a one-off setup price + a separate recurring monthly price
- Create an **Invoice**: add the customer, select the product (both prices show on the invoice), add memo, set payment window (30 days)
- Send the invoice — customer pays via the Stripe-hosted link (credit card, PayPal, Google Pay, Apple Pay)
- Customise branding: add your logo + brand colour to the invoice

### The invoice experience

- Customer receives an email with a payment link
- Looks professional (same system used by Midjourney, ChatGPT etc.)
- Once they pay: they're on a monthly subscription automatically

## Step 5 — Post-Launch & Ongoing Management

### Connecting their domain (two routes)

1. **Point name servers at their current registrar** (most common) — client keeps their existing domain, you update the DNS to point to Horizons. Hostinger has a step-by-step video guide for this.
2. **Register a new domain via Hostinger** — client gets a brand new domain. Click the 3 dots on the site → Change Domain → search → register.

### Tip on domain access

- Ask clients for their registrar login so you can do the DNS update yourself
- Clients don't know what DNS is. If you leave it to them, it won't happen.

### Website monitoring (Uptimerobot)

- Free service: add all client websites, it monitors uptime 24/7
- Sends email alerts when a site goes down
- **Key feature**: sends monthly uptime reports that you can forward directly to clients
- Positions you as professional and proactive without doing any extra work

### Monthly value delivery

- Forward the Uptimerobot monthly report to each client
- Optionally: do basic SEO updates, content refreshes
- This is how you justify the $39+/month and reduce churn

## The Build Workflow

### Step 1 — Find a reference site + scrape it

> *"I want you to build a website that's similar to [URL]. Run it on my local host for now."*
> 

Claude scrapes the site, builds a similar version on localhost. You tweak before connecting to Vercel/Supabase.

**Important**: don't copy exactly. Innovate from the reference. Change fonts, colours, layout decisions. Make it better. This is how every brand works — study what works, then improve it.

### Step 2 — Test locally first

Everything runs on `localhost:8080` until you're happy. You don't need Vercel/Supabase/domain until you're ready to show a client.

- Claude Code gives you the exact values to enter (Type, Host, Value)
- Have clients pay for their own domains once you've signed them

Open Claude chat, use voice or text:

> *"Find restaurants in [city] that don't have a website."*

Claude opens Chrome, searches Google Maps, clicks through each listing one by one, checks for a website, reports back. Completely hands-off lead research.

# 🎥 Video 4 — Why AI Websites Are a Trap (And the High-Ticket Alternative) — Carson

**Channel:** Carson | **Focus:** Contrarian take on AI website model, high-ticket AI agency with lead gen + voice AI

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8FysaFHj79U

## The Core Argument Against Selling AI Websites

Selling AI websites to local businesses at $100–200/month is a volume game that almost nobody wins. To make real money at that price point, you need hundreds or thousands of clients. The top 0.01% get there. The 99.9% don't.

**Three reasons it fails in practice:**

- You're competing on price, not value — the service doesn't move the needle for the business
- Cheap clients don't care about you, are likely to dispute or refund, and churn fast
- You're selling a vanity metric (a nice website) not revenue

> "A website is cool to have, but it's not the needle mover."
> 

## The Alternative: Intentionally Small, High-Ticket

Work with fewer clients who pay a lot more because you're solving a real revenue problem. One client at $10K–25K is infinitely easier than 100 clients at $200/month.

**Rule**: only target industries where the business owner makes serious money and where a missed lead has real dollar cost. Avoid razor-margin businesses (restaurants, nail salons).

### Best industries (in three buckets)

- **Home services**: roofing, HVAC, solar, landscaping
- **Medical services**: med spa, dental, GLP-1 clinics, men's health clinics, stem cell clinics
- **Professional services**: accounting, law firms, real estate agents

**How to choose**: pick based on personal experience or context. If your dad is a roofer, go after roofers. If you're studying law, go after lawyers. Stick with one industry for 3–6 months minimum — switching niches resets everything.

## What These Businesses Actually Struggle With

1. **Missing calls** — a roofing company misses a call = ~$8,000 average job lost
2. **Poor advertising** — they're 40–60 years old and don't understand social media or digital ads
3. **Overworked owners** — no time for the leverage activities that would actually grow the business

These three problems = a high-ticket offer that solves real revenue gaps.

## The Two-Part AI System to Sell

### Part 1 — Facebook & Instagram Ads (Lead Generation)

- Run social media ads for the client to generate inbound leads
- Use **Arc Ads** (arcads.ai) to create the ad creatives with AI actors
    - Give it a 30–45 second script (write with ChatGPT or Claude)
    - Pick from a library of AI actors — they read the script on video
    - ~$10 per video, takes 20 minutes to make
    - Performing better than human UGC creators in many cases recently
- This is the SMMA (social media marketing agency) model, which has existed for years — now supercharged with AI-generated video creatives

### Part 2 — AI Voice Caller (Lead → Appointment)

- Leads that come in through ads need to be called within 5 minutes to convert
- Use **Retell.ai** for AI voice receptionists
    - 14 cents per minute per call
    - No coding required — prompt the agent via their platform
    - Calls every new lead automatically, books them into the client's calendar
    - One-and-done setup; minimal maintenance after launch

**Combined**: ads generate leads → AI caller converts them to booked appointments → client closes the deal. This is the full revenue bridge.

## Pricing

- Beginner: $3,000–5,000 upfront setup fee
- Standard: $5,000–10,000 upfront
- Carson's agency: $15,000–25,000 upfront

Ongoing: maintenance is roughly 1 hour/week (refreshing ad creatives when they fatigue). The AI caller is set-and-forget.

## The Core Positioning Principle

> "The easiest way to make yourself rich is by making others rich."
> 

When you bring a client revenue, they become dependent on your system. You're not competing on price anymore — you're competing on value. And value is open-ended.

## Apply to Your Situation

- **If you're building apps**, this business model is a better parallel revenue stream than selling AI websites — the margin is higher, the clients are better, and the model scales without needing hundreds of clients
- **Arc Ads** — even if you're not running an agency, AI actor video ads at $10/video is a useful tool for any app launch or product marketing. Worth knowing exists.
- **Retell.ai** — voice AI at 14 cents/minute. If any of your apps (JobRunner, Criti, Kindred) ever need a voice interaction layer or client-facing phone system, this is the platform to know
- **The "compete on value not price" principle** — directly relevant to how you price your apps. Low-priced subscriptions attract users who don't care. Higher pricing with clear ROI attracts users who are invested.
- **Niche specificity** — Carson's argument for staying in one industry (productised fulfillment) applies to app development too. JobRunner for job seekers is stronger than JobRunner for everyone.

## The Outreach System

### Warm outreach (Instagram DMs)

- Search local businesses on Instagram
- Look for accounts with 500–5,000 followers with a website link in bio
- Click the link — if it looks like 2015, that's your prospect
- **Do NOT DM saying "I can build you a website"** — ignored 99 times out of 100

**Instead — do the work first:**

1. Take 20 minutes, build their homepage with their logo, colours, product photos

## The 5-Step Launch Framework (Applicable to Any of These Models)

### Step 1 — Validate

### Step 5 — Productise
