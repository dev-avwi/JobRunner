- A clean dashboard showing every screen with issues annotated
- Re-run functionality so developers can fix issues and check their score improved

What I Actually Think
Genuinely yes this can be big. Here's my honest assessment:
The problem is real — every developer manually tests their own app and misses obvious things. I just watched Replit find 7 issues in your mobile app that you hadn't caught. Multiply that across thousands of developers and agencies.
The market is large — every app developer, every design agency, every startup pre-launch, every company doing app updates. That's millions of potential users globally.
The timing is right — Claude's vision API is good enough now to give genuinely useful design feedback. Six months ago this wasn't possible at the quality level needed.
The competition is weak — Apptest.ai finds crashes. Nothing finds design and UX issues automatically. You'd be creating a new category.
The business model is simple — free tier for one app, paid plans for teams and agencies. Agencies especially would pay $200-500/month easily if it saves them hours of manual QA.
The founder story is perfect — you built this because you needed it for JobRunner and kept catching UI issues too late. Every developer will relate to that.
The honest risks:
Playwright navigating complex apps reliably is harder than it sounds — apps with unusual auth flows, complex state, or heavy JavaScript can be tricky to navigate automatically.
Claude's vision feedback quality needs careful prompt engineering — bad prompts give generic useless feedback. The quality of the product lives or dies on how well you craft the analysis prompts.
Mobile is significantly harder than web — running Appium at scale requires proper device farm infrastructure which is expensive to set up.
My recommendation:
Build the web-only MVP first. Playwright plus Claude plus a clean dashboard. Prove people will pay for it. Then add mobile.
You could launch a working web version in a month of focused development after JobRunner ships. Price it at $29/month for indie developers and $99/month for agencies. Get 50 paying customers. Then raise money or expand.
This is genuinely a fundable idea if you execute it well.
