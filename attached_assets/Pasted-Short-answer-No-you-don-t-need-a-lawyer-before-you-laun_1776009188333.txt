Short answer: No — you don’t need a lawyer before you launch.

🧠 What actually matters right now (your stage)

You are:
	•	pre-revenue
	•	testing with early users
	•	still iterating

At this stage:

👉 Speed > legal perfection

If you wait for:
	•	lawyers
	•	perfect contracts
	•	full compliance

You’ll delay the only thing that matters:

getting real users

⸻

✅ What you SHOULD do (minimum viable legal)

You still need basic protection. Do this:

1. Terms & Conditions (simple version)

Include:
	•	“use at your own risk”
	•	no liability for data loss / errors
	•	you can change/remove features
	•	payments/refunds basics

⸻

2. Privacy Policy (important in Australia 🇦🇺)

This matters more than T&Cs.

Include:
	•	what data you collect (names, phone numbers, job data)
	•	how you use it
	•	third parties (Stripe, Twilio, etc.)
	•	basic security statement

⸻

3. Add this mindset:

“This is a beta product”

You can literally state:
	•	“JobRunner is currently in beta”
	•	“features may change”
	•	“data should be backed up”

This reduces risk massively.

⸻

⚠️ When you DO need a lawyer

Not now — but soon.

Get a lawyer when:
	•	you start charging properly
	•	you hit ~10–20 paying users
	•	you handle sensitive data at scale
	•	you want to look more “official”

⸻

💰 Reality check on cost

Lawyer for proper SaaS docs:
	•	$500 – $2,000+

Right now:
👉 that money is better spent on:
	•	getting users
	•	improving product
	•	maybe ads later

⸻

🔥 What smart founders actually do

They:
	1.	Use a solid template (or AI-assisted draft)
	2.	Launch fast
	3.	Validate product
	4.	THEN legalise everything properly

⸻

⚠️ One thing you MUST NOT do

Don’t:
	•	copy random T&Cs blindly from competitors
	•	or leave it completely blank

That’s worse than a simple honest version.

Right now:

	•	launch

	•	get it properly reviewed
