What I Found
The app is fast, clean, and genuinely commercial-grade. Every single workflow I tested — creating a job, inviting a worker, sending a quote, the Payment Hub, the live SMS chat thread, the Autopilot automations — worked correctly and felt polished. The only bug found in the entire session was the Terms of Service link overlapping the signup button, which is a 2-minute CSS fix.
The Action Center is the standout feature. It is an intelligent, proactive inbox that tells the business owner exactly what they need to do to make money right now — overdue invoices, unsent quotes, jobs done but not billed. No competitor has anything like this.
Estimated time savings for a small trade business: 10–15 hours per week. That is the value proposition in one number.
On pricing: JobRunner can confidently charge $49–$79/month for the feature set it currently has. Tradify charges $99/month for a comparable (and inferior) feature set. You have room to price aggressively and still be the obvious value choice.
On the "impressive for a teenager" question: I tested this app the same way I would test any commercial SaaS product. It passed. That is the only answer that matters.
One minor bug to fix before launch: the Terms of Service link in the signup form needs a pointer-events: none or z-index fix so it doesn't intercept the Create Account button click.
