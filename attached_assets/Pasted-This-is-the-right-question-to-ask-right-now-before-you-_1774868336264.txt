The Full Journey Map for JobRunner
The Things That Will Make or Break It
1. Distribution is the only thing that matters right now.
The product is built. Every hour you spend adding features instead of getting paying customers is an hour wasted. The app already has more features than Tradify had when it hit $1M ARR. You do not need more features — you need 20 tradies using it daily and telling their mates about it. Word of mouth in the trades is the most powerful marketing channel that exists. One happy sparky in Brisbane tells his apprentice, who tells his dad, who tells his footy team. That is how Tradify grew. That is how you grow.
2. The mobile apps need to be live before any serious push.
Everything else can wait. The App Store and Google Play listings are the single most important unfinished task. A tradie who cannot find your app by searching "JobRunner" on their phone will not trust the product, full stop. This is not a nice-to-have — it is a prerequisite for any real marketing effort.
3. Churn will kill you faster than anything else.
Getting 20 customers is hard. Keeping them is harder. The number one reason tradies churn from software is that it "got too complicated" or "I just stopped using it." Your onboarding is already strong, but you need to watch what happens at Day 7, Day 14, and Day 30. If a user has not created a job and sent a quote within their first 7 days, they will almost certainly never pay. Build an automated email sequence that nudges them at those milestones — "Hey Dave, you haven't sent your first quote yet — here's how to do it in 2 minutes."
4. Pricing needs to go up before you scale.
At $39/month you are signalling "beta project." At $59/month you are signalling "professional tool." The difference in revenue at 500 customers is $10,000/month. More importantly, higher-priced customers churn less — they have committed more and they take the product more seriously. Raise prices for new customers the moment the mobile apps are live and the critical bugs from the audit are fixed.
The Legal and Compliance Things Nobody Talks About
ABN and GST registration. Once you cross $75,000 AUD in annual revenue you are legally required to register for GST. At $10K MRR you are at $120K ARR — well above the threshold. Get this sorted early so it does not become a problem later.
Privacy Policy and Terms of Service. The current ones need to be reviewed by a lawyer before you have real paying customers. Specifically, you are storing GPS location data, financial data (invoices, payments), and business communications. In Australia, the Privacy Act 1988 applies to businesses handling personal information. A proper privacy policy is not just legal protection — it is a trust signal for tradies who are handing over their client lists.
Stripe and financial services. Because you are processing payments on behalf of other businesses via Stripe Connect, you are operating as a payment facilitator. Stripe handles most of the compliance, but you need to ensure your Terms of Service clearly state that you are not a financial institution and that Stripe is the payment processor. This protects you if a payment dispute arises.
Data residency. Australian businesses increasingly care about where their data is stored. If your database is hosted in the US (common with Replit/Neon Postgres), you should either move to an Australian AWS or Google Cloud region, or clearly disclose the data location. This will come up in enterprise sales conversations.
The Technical Debt That Will Bite You at Scale
The audit identified these in detail, but in plain terms the three things that will cause real problems as you grow are: the 4.45MB single JavaScript bundle (your app will load slowly on mobile networks in regional Australia — Cairns, Darwin, Townsville — where tradies actually work), the lack of rate limiting on the login endpoint (one angry ex-employee or competitor could lock out your entire user base), and the polling-based real-time updates (at 500 concurrent businesses all polling every 30 seconds, your server will struggle). None of these are urgent today, but all three need to be on a roadmap for the next 6 months.
The Competitive Moat — What to Build Next
Once the basics are solid, the features that will make JobRunner genuinely difficult to compete with are the ones that create switching costs. The more deeply a tradie's business data lives inside JobRunner, the harder it is for them to leave. The features that create the deepest switching costs are:
The AI Receptionist call history and lead tracking — once a tradie has 6 months of inbound call data showing which suburbs generate the most work, they will never want to start that data collection over again somewhere else.
The client relationship history — every quote, every job, every invoice, every SMS, every photo, all attached to a client record. After 12 months of real use, that history is irreplaceable.
The financial reporting — once a tradie's accountant is pulling their Xero-synced reports from JobRunner, switching means disrupting their accounting workflow, which no tradie will do voluntarily.
Build these three things deeply and you have a product that is genuinely sticky at the business level, not just the individual user level.
The Partnership That Could Change Everything
Xero has an App Marketplace with over 1,000 apps. Getting listed on the Xero App Marketplace is the single highest-leverage distribution move available to you. Xero has over 3 million subscribers globally and approximately 1 million in Australia. Their marketplace gets hundreds of thousands of visits per month from small business owners actively looking for tools to connect to their accounting. A listing there, with a few genuine reviews, could generate more trials per month than any paid advertising campaign.
The requirement for listing is a certified Xero integration — which means token refresh must be bulletproof, the sync must be two-way, and you need to pass Xero's certification checklist. This is a 4–6 week engineering project but the payoff is enormous.
The Mindset Shift That Matters Most
Right now you are thinking like a developer — "what should I build next?" The shift that needs to happen is to start thinking like a founder — "who is my next customer and how do I get them?" Those are completely different questions and they require completely different daily habits.
The most important thing you can do this week is not write a single line of code. It is to find 5 tradies, show them the app, watch them use it without helping them, and listen to every moment of confusion or delight. That 2 hours of observation will tell you more about what to build next than any amount of technical analysis.
The product is ready enough. The journey from here is a sales and distribution problem, not an engineering problem.
