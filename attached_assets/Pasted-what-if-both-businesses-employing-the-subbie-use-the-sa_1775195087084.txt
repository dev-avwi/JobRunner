Great edge case and honestly a really elegant solution exists for this.

The Scenario

Subbie Jake works for Mike's Construction and Dave's Plumbing. Both businesses use JobRunner. Jake has one JobRunner account with two business workspaces in it.

How It Should Work

Jake's single account holds both workspace connections. When Mike assigns Jake a job, it appears in Jake's "Mike's Construction" workspace. When Dave assigns Jake a job, it appears in Jake's "Dave's Plumbing" workspace.

The location isolation still applies exactly the same way — Mike only sees Jake when Jake has an active Mike's Construction job running. Dave only sees Jake when Jake has an active Dave's Plumbing job running. Neither can see the other's jobs or Jake's location outside their own active jobs.

The Clever Part

Because both businesses are on JobRunner, Jake gets a genuinely seamless experience. One app. One login. Two workspaces. No confusion about which app is for which business.

And here's the thing that's actually a competitive advantage — this becomes a network effect. Mike uses JobRunner and invites his subbies. Those subbies are now on JobRunner. Dave sees his subbies already have JobRunner accounts and it's frictionless to bring them onto his workflow too. Dave signs up for JobRunner because his subbies are already on it.

That's exactly how Slack grew. One team adopts it, their contacts already have accounts, the network expands organically.
