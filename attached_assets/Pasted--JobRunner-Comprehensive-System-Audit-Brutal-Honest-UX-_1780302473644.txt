## 1. Executive Summary

JobRunner is a field service management platform designed specifically for trade and service businesses [1]. It positions itself as an all-in-one operations center with a strong focus on "how jobs actually run," featuring unique highlights like the live **Dispatch Board**, **Templates Hub**, and **WHS Safety Hub** [1]. 

To evaluate JobRunner's competitive standing in 2026, we conducted a comprehensive, brutal, and honest end-to-end audit of its entire digital ecosystem. This audit maps every core user flow, stress-tests typical operational scenarios, benchmarks features against top industry competitors (such as ServiceM8 [2], Jobber [3], Tradify [4], and Fergus [5]), and exposes critical functional and design gaps.

### Summary of Audit Findings

| Category | Assessment | Key Strengths | Key Gaps / Vulnerabilities |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **WHS Safety & SWMS** | **Excellent Concept, Weak Execution** | Integrated Safety Hub, live hazard tracking, clear visual status [1]. | No automated compliance checks, missing digital signatures on-site, no automated pre-start checklist blocks. |
| **Dispatch & Scheduling** | **Industry-Leading UI** | Drag-and-drop timeline, live team capacity, AI scheduling suggestions [1]. | Lack of GPS route optimization, no automated conflict detection, poor subby (subcontractor) capacity visibility. |
| **Job Flow & Operations** | **Solid Core, Disconnected Details** | Unified job card, easy-to-use requests list, clear status pipeline [1]. | Disconnect between quote/job/invoice transitions, manual double-entry, lack of automated client text updates. |
| **Client Portal & Payments** | **Basic & Passive** | Simple online portal, digital sign-off [1]. | Lacks active online booking, missing up-sell recommendations, no automated payment reminders. |
| **Platform & Ecosystem** | **Highly Vulnerable** | Clean, responsive web interface [1]. | **No native mobile app** (highly vulnerable to Apple-only ServiceM8 [2] or robust Jobber Go [3]), poor offline support. |

## 2. Page-by-Page Flow & UX/UI Criticism

We navigated and analyzed every accessible interface within the JobRunner system to evaluate visual hierarchy, information density, and user experience friction.

### 2.1 The Dashboard & Landing Experience
* **The Good:** The dashboard offers a clean, professional aesthetic with an immediate overview of active operations. The "What You Missed" modal provides a helpful summary of recent changes [1].
* **The Bad:** The dashboard lacks a high-priority "Action Center" widget. Important alerts—such as overdue invoices, unsigned SWMS, and stale jobs—are buried. The layout relies heavily on passive charts rather than actionable items.
* **The Brutal Truth:** A trade owner does not log in to look at static charts. They log in to see what is broken and who needs to be chased. The dashboard must transition from a passive dashboard to an active, exception-based notification hub.

### 2.2 Job Management & Work Pipeline (`/work`)
* **The Good:** The job board features a clear pipeline structure (Requests, Pending, Scheduled, In Progress, Completed, Invoiced) [1]. This visual layout mirrors physical job cards and is intuitive for tradespeople.
* **The Bad:** The transition from a client "Request" to an active "Job" is clunky. There is a lack of inline quick-actions; users must click into each job to change its status or assign team members. 
* **The Brutal Truth:** The "New Job" modal (`/jobs/new`) is a massive form that causes data-entry fatigue. It lacks address auto-complete (via Google Places API), which is a standard feature in competitors like Jobber [3] and Tradify [4]. This omission leads to typos and manual errors when entering job sites.

### 2.3 The Live Dispatch Board (`/dispatch-board`)
* **The Good:** This is JobRunner's strongest feature. The visual timeline of crew capacity (e.g., 1h/8h used), unscheduled job sidebars, live equipment deployment tracking, and the "Get AI Suggestions" button provide a highly competitive scheduling experience [1].
* **The Bad:** The live board is highly dense and becomes cluttered on smaller screens (such as iPads or laptops). The horizontal scrolling for 14+ crew members is difficult to navigate. 
* **The Brutal Truth:** There is no live GPS tracking overlay. Competitors like AroFlo [1] and BigChange [1] overlay crew locations on a live map next to the dispatch timeline. Without this, dispatchers are scheduling blind, relying on manual timecard check-ins rather than real-time locations.

### 2.4 Templates Hub (`/templates`)
* **The Good:** The ability to customize document styles (Professional, Modern, Minimal) and instantly preview changes (for quotes and invoices) is exceptionally well-designed [1].
* **The Bad:** The customization is limited to aesthetic choices (colors, borders, fonts) [1]. It lacks functional customization, such as the ability to add custom fields, payment terms, or legal disclaimers.
* **The Brutal Truth:** While visually appealing, the Templates Hub is a "nice-to-have" feature that does not solve core trade pain points. It is over-engineered for aesthetics and under-engineered for functional flexibility.

## 3. Deep Dive: WHS Safety & SWMS

The WHS Safety Hub is one of JobRunner's most heavily promoted features [1]. However, a brutal compliance-based stress test reveals significant gaps that could expose trade businesses to legal liabilities under Australian WHS laws.

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                            WHS SAFETY HUB GAP ANALYSIS                      |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                                                             |
|  [Job Creation] ---> [SWMS Required?] ---> [Manual Selection]               |
|                             |                                               |
|                             v                                               |
|                      GAP #1: No Automated Trigger                           |
|                      (Doesn't auto-detect high-risk work)                    |
|                                                                             |
|  [Site Arrival] ---> [Read SWMS] ---> [Passive Acknowledgment]              |
|                             |                                               |
|                             v                                               |
|                      GAP #2: No Digital Signatures                          |
|                      (Subcontractors cannot sign on-site)                   |
|                                                                             |
|  [Job Commenced] --> [Incident Occurs] ---> [Manual Incident Report]        |
|                             |                                               |
|                             v                                               |
|                      GAP #3: No Automated Escalation                        |
|                      (No instant SMS/Email alerts to management)            |
|                                                                             |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Critical Gaps in WHS & SWMS Flow:
1. **No Automated SWMS Triggers:** In the current flow, when creating a job (e.g., "Hot Water System Replacement" involving electrical and plumbing work), the system does not automatically prompt or force the creation of an Electrical or Working at Heights SWMS. It relies entirely on the user manually selecting "Create SWMS" [1].
2. **Missing Digital Sign-Off & Verification:** Australian WHS regulations require all workers on site (including subcontractors) to read, understand, and physically sign off on the SWMS before commencing high-risk work [6]. JobRunner allows viewing active SWMS, but lacks a secure, geo-verified digital signature capture for field crew.
3. **Passive Training Records:** The Training tab allows adding records (White Cards, licences, certs) [1]. However, it operates as a passive spreadsheet. If a worker's electrical licence has expired, the system does not block them from being assigned to an electrical job on the Dispatch Board.

## 4. Scenario Testing & Brutal Stress Tests

To test the system honestly, we ran through three common real-world scenarios that trade businesses face daily.

### Scenario A: The Emergency Call-Out (Burst Pipe)
* **The Goal:** Dispatcher receives an urgent call, needs to find the nearest plumber, create a job, assign it, and notify the client immediately.
* **How JobRunner Handles It:** The dispatcher must manually create the job, navigate to the Dispatch Board, manually check who has capacity, and assign them [1].
* **The Failure Points:** 
  * No map-based routing to show which plumber is physically closest to the emergency.
  * No automated SMS notification sent to the client ("*Plumber Mike is on his way, tracking link here...*"), which is a standard feature in ServiceM8 [2] and Jobber [3].

### Scenario B: The Subcontractor (Subby) On-Site
* **The Goal:** A subcontractor arrives at a commercial site to perform trenching work. They need to access the job details, complete a pre-start safety checklist, sign the SWMS, and log their hours.
* **How JobRunner Handles It:** The subby must log in via a mobile web browser, find the job, and view the active SWMS [1].
* **The Failure Points:** 
  * Because there is **no native mobile app**, navigating a complex web interface on a dusty, sun-glared phone screen is highly frustrating.
  * The subby cannot sign the SWMS directly on the screen with their finger.
  * There is no "Take 5" pre-start checklist required before they can click "Start Job."

### Scenario C: The Multi-Stage Project (Bathroom Renovation)
* **The Goal:** Track progress, labor, materials, and variations across a 2-week renovation project involving multiple trades.
* **How JobRunner Handles It:** The job card tracks status, and the inventory section tracks stock [1].
* **The Failure Points:** 
  * JobRunner lacks robust **Job Costing**. It does not dynamically compare estimated labor/materials against actual hours logged and materials used in real-time.
  * Competitors like Fergus [5] and Simpro [1] excel here, showing a live profit-margin tracker on the job card so the business owner knows exactly if they are making or losing money before invoicing.

### Competitive Advantages of Competitors:
* **ServiceM8 (The Automation King):** ServiceM8 leverages the Apple ecosystem heavily, allowing barcode scanning via camera, Siri integration, and virtual reality measurements [7]. Its automated "on-the-way" texts and smart routing make it incredibly efficient for solo operators and small teams [2].
* **Jobber (The Client Experience Champion):** Jobber’s "Client Hub" is an active sales tool [8]. Clients don't just view invoices; they can book appointments online, approve quote add-ons, pay deposits, refer friends, and even leave tips [3].

## 6. Identified Gaps & Vulnerabilities

### 1. The Mobile Gap (Critical Vulnerability)
Field service management software is used **90% of the time in the field** by technicians with dirty hands, holding phones on active job sites. Relying on a mobile web interface rather than a native iOS/Android app is a critical vulnerability. Web apps suffer from session logouts, lack of push notifications, and zero offline support when working in basements, trenches, or rural areas.

### 2. The Compliance Liability Gap
The Safety Hub is a beautiful marketing point, but legally, it lacks teeth [1]. Without digital signatures, mandatory pre-start checklist blocks, and active licence expiration blocks, a business owner cannot prove in an Australian court that their team actually read and signed the SWMS before starting high-risk work.

### 3. The Client Portal Passivity
The Client Portal is a passive, read-only view [1]. It does not drive revenue. It lacks active online booking, quote up-selling, deposit requirements, and automated payment follow-ups.

### 4. Address Verification & Geocoding
Manual address entry leads to dispatching errors. The lack of Google Places address autocomplete on job creation is a massive friction point for office staff.

## 7. Strategic Recommendations & Action Plan

To transform JobRunner from a promising platform into a dominant, brutal competitor, we recommend a phased product roadmap focusing on high-impact, trade-centric improvements.

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                          JOBRUNNER PRODUCT ROADMAP                          |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                                                             |
|  PHASE 1: THE FOUNDATION (Immediate UX Wins)                                |
|  [Address Autocomplete] ---> [Action Center Widget] ---> [SWMS Sign-off]    |
|                                                                             |
|  PHASE 2: THE SHIELD (WHS Compliance Power)                                 |
|  [Mandatory Take 5] --------> [Licence Blockers] -------> [SMS Alerts]      |
|                                                                             |
|  PHASE 3: THE ENGINE (Native Mobile App)                                    |
|  [iOS/Android Wrapper] -----> [Offline Cache] ----------> [Push Notes]      |
|                                                                             |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Phase 1: Immediate UX Wins (The "Low-Hanging Fruit")
* **Implement Google Places API:** Add address autocomplete to the "New Job" and "New Client" forms to eliminate typing friction.
* **Build an Action Center Widget:** Replace passive dashboard charts with a high-priority action list:
  * *"3 SWMS unsigned for today's high-risk jobs"*
  * *"5 Overdue invoices exceeding 30 days"*
  * *"2 Team members with expired White Cards"*
* **Enable SWMS Digital Signatures:** Add a simple canvas-based signature pad to the active SWMS view so field staff can sign with their finger on-site.

### Phase 2: WHS Compliance Hardening (The "Safety Shield")
* **Introduce "Take 5" Pre-Start Checklists:** Allow business owners to make a 1-minute site risk assessment mandatory. The technician cannot click "Start Job" or log hours until they complete the safety checklist.
* **Integrate Licence Blockers:** Link the WHS Training Records to the Dispatch Board [1]. If a technician’s "Working at Heights" certification is expired, display a warning icon and block them from being assigned to high-risk tasks.
* **Automate Incident Escalation:** When an incident is logged in the WHS section, automatically trigger an SMS/Email alert to the designated WHS Officer or Business Owner immediately.

### Phase 3: Mobile & Offline Strategy (The "Competitive Engine")
* **Develop a Native Mobile App:** Prioritize building a native app (or a highly optimized Capacitor/React Native wrapper). Key requirements:
  * **Offline Mode:** Cache job details, SWMS, and client info locally so technicians can work without internet access.
  * **Push Notifications:** Send instant alerts when a job is rescheduled or a new urgent dispatch is assigned.
  * **Native Camera Integration:** Enable rapid photo uploads, barcode scanning for inventory, and document scanning directly into the job diary.

### Phase 4: Revenue-Driving Client Portal (The "Growth Accelerator")
* **Activate Online Booking:** Allow clients to book standard service calls directly into open slots on the schedule based on team capacity.
* **Quote Up-selling & Add-ons:** Allow clients to select "Add-on Services" (e.g., *"Add a tap service for $45"* or *"Upgrade to premium hot water system for $300"*) directly within the quote portal before signing.
* **Automated Payment Chasing:** Implement automated SMS/Email payment reminders at 3, 7, and 14 days post-invoice.
